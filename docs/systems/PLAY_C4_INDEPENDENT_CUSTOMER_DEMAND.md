## Contract

- `service.demand.configure` is a reviewed `system:director` command accepting an explicit `plan_id`, NPC, venue, recipe, route, initial world minute, recurrence interval (0 means one-shot), waiting minutes, and probability in integer basis points (0–10,000). All input IDs, NPC/venue/recipe references, route capacity, future due time, non-overlapping wait/interval and overflow bounds are checked **by P4**. C0's authoring whitelist allows the same command in future versioned world bundles. No hardcoded starting customers, price, rent, chance, venue, or clock speed.
- The command registers **two domain-owned recurring P1 scheduler jobs**: NPC intent at priority 250 and corresponding expiry at priority 300. P2 scheduled NPC departures have priority 100 and arrivals 200, so the demand sees the NPC's **actual living room/location at the fire minute**; it is never gated on player presence, proximity or game camera. Schedule job IDs/topics are reserved from generic schedule.create/cancel/reschedule commands.
- A due policy considers actual NPC presence, existing active order for that NPC, route capacity, current per-occurrence intent and a reproducible SHA-256 seeded decision (`seed:plan:occurrence` modulo 10,000, with exact 0% and 100% boundaries). This is a **bounded deterministic intent policy**, not learning AI, character dialogue or a generic population simulation. It creates an ordinary P4 `offered` order in the same world-time event, with an original, deterministic `demand:<plan>:<occurrence>` ID and stable scheduler provenance. It does **not** grant cash.
- Each offer has an **already-scheduled deadline**, so one 50-minute clock jump and fifty single-minute jumps both create/expire the same order at the proper 6/26-minute boundaries. A skipped offer's expiry is a harmless no-op; expired or departed customers cannot be accepted or paid, and recurring policy timers aren't accidentally cancelled when one order closes. The full outcome—including skipped outcomes/reasons—is included in `time.advanced` effect and independently re-derived and validated on replay. The existing P4 payment and delivery boundaries still apply unchanged.
- The only added authoritative state is domain-owned scheduler jobs and ordinary P4 orders in **existing WorldKernel v8 fields**; no parallel timer, JSON scene-owned offers or speculative schema migration. Existing v7→v8 support and saves are unaffected if no demand policy was configured.

## QA scenario, evidence and limits

`content/service/qa_autonomous_demand.v1.json` is an **explicit QA-only plan**: visitor Reda, kiosk counter, tea, first offer minute 6, 20-minute wait, optional next-day recurrence and deterministic 100% intention. This does **not** assert what a real customer wants, how frequently they visit or what Egyptian money is worth. `scripts/integration/customer_demand_config.gd` only loads packaged policy JSON and dispatches to the canonical kernel. A separate `scenes/qa_autonomous_kiosk.tscn` opts in to this plan and its own Web save slot, reusing the existing C1/C2/C3 walkable scene script. All other QA scenes and the visual PR #20 remain unchanged.

`tests/test_play_c4.gd` runs the P0–P7/C0 simulation with the player staying on the roof while Reda arrives at the kiosk and independently offers a customer order; verifies player cannot accept remotely, expiry at its authored time, offscreen return, no invented money, repeat attempt skipped after NPC departure, 0% customer intent, illegal policy/job mutation rejected, large/small tick equivalence of order and scheduler state, exact deterministic imports, saved mid-offer restoration and malicious final-state rejection. `tools/web-play-c4-smoke.mjs` tests actual Godot Web scene in Chromium, with no O key: NPC visit at minute 5, order at minute 6, unattended expiry at minute 26, NPC departure at minute 35, no remote acceptance, and **two full Web reloads preserving the entire world SHA-256 digest**. These tests run within the pre-existing native/Web workflows alongside C3, P7 and D001.

**Not implemented:** NPC desire or preference formation through feelings/social memories; customer arrival animation, menu displays, general content population, crowd collision; production pricing; final visual art; emergent recipe choice. Demand policies must be authored and verified per NPC for now; real balancing and authored content still remain.
